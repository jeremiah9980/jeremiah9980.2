⚠ Wire Fraud — Why This Is the Number-One Finding for a Title Company

Real-estate wire fraud is not a theoretical risk. It is one of the most common and fastest-growing cybercrime categories in the United States, and title companies sit at the exact pressure point attackers target — the moment a buyer is wiring closing funds. A domain without DMARC enforcement is the direct enabler of this attack.

$275M

lost to real-estate fraud in 2025 — up from $173M in 2024 and $145M in 2023

12,368

real-estate fraud complaints to FBI IC3 in 2025 — ~34 per day

$3.04B

Business Email Compromise losses in 2025 — the mechanism behind most closing-wire fraud

The scale of the problem
  • Real-estate fraud losses grew from $145M in 2023 → $173M in 2024 → $275M in 2025 — a 59% year-over-year jump, with complaints rising from 9,359 to 12,368.
  • Over the past decade, annual losses from real-estate wire fraud have grown roughly 50-fold — from about $9M to hundreds of millions per year.
  • Business Email Compromise — the umbrella that includes almost every real-estate wire fraud case — drove $3.04B in losses across 24,768 complaints in 2025, the second-largest cybercrime loss category reported to the FBI.
  • AI-assisted impersonation drove more than 22,000 complaints and $893M in losses in 2025, making spoofed emails harder to spot than ever.
Sources: FBI IC3 2025 Internet Crime Report (released April 2026) · NAR · HousingWire · CertifID
