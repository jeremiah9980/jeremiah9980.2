What this means for real estate teams

If your company handles closings, wire instructions, contracts, title communication, or earnest-money conversations, your domain reputation is part of the trust chain.

SPF

Who is allowed to send?

SPF tells mail systems which servers are authorized to send for your domain. Weak or bloated SPF records can make verification less reliable.

DKIM

Was the message signed?

DKIM adds a cryptographic signature to legitimate outbound mail. Missing or misaligned DKIM can make it harder to prove the message is authentic.

DMARC

What should receivers do?

DMARC ties SPF and DKIM together and tells receivers whether to monitor, quarantine, or reject messages that fail authentication.

Suggested next steps

Start with visibility, then move carefully toward enforcement so legitimate email keeps flowing while spoofed email gets blocked.

1

Scan the domain you actually send from.

Check your primary company domain plus any domains used by marketing, transaction systems, CRM tools, or closing platforms.

2

Verify SPF and DKIM alignment.

Confirm that Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, website forms, CRMs, and email marketing tools are authorized and properly signing mail.

3

Move DMARC beyond monitoring.

Use reporting first, fix legitimate senders, then stage DMARC from p=none toward p=quarantine or p=reject.

4

Train staff on wire-change verification.

Email authentication reduces impersonation risk, but wire instructions should still be verified through known trusted phone numbers and internal procedures.
