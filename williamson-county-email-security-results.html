Collection approach

I treated this as Williamson County, Texas and built a practical seed list across real estate brokerages/agents, title/escrow, property management, mortgage, builders, developers, apartment/community operators, and related local services with public-facing domains.

The strongest public sources were local chamber/member directories, REALTOR-related directories, and title-company office/location pages. The list was biased toward domains useful for outreach or SPF, DKIM, and DMARC scans rather than generic listing portals.

Status legend

Use this color code when reviewing individual company domains or sharing remediation status with leadership, IT vendors, title teams, or real estate offices.

Status Plain-English meaning Typical finding Recommended action
Red / Failed Domain is exposed to obvious impersonation risk indicators. DMARC missing or not enforcing, SPF/DKIM issues, or alignment gaps. Fix DNS records, validate legitimate senders, then move DMARC toward quarantine/reject.
Yellow / Some Checks Fail Some protection exists, but the domain is not fully hardened. SPF exists but is messy, DKIM incomplete, or DMARC is monitor-only. Clean up SPF, enable DKIM everywhere, review reports, then tighten DMARC policy.
Green / Good Domain shows stronger email-authentication posture. SPF/DKIM aligned and DMARC policy is meaningfully protective. Keep monitoring reports and re-check after adding new CRM, marketing, or closing systems.

What each check means

These are the core records that help stop email that only looks like it came from a trusted company domain.

SPF

Who is allowed to send?

SPF tells mail systems which servers are authorized to send for the domain. Bad SPF can allow confusion or cause legitimate systems to fail authentication.

DKIM

Was it signed?

DKIM signs legitimate outbound mail. Missing DKIM can weaken proof that an email truly came from the company’s approved systems.

DMARC

What should receivers do?

DMARC connects SPF and DKIM alignment and tells receiving systems whether to monitor, quarantine, or reject failed mail.
